=== INDEX 77 ===
Your survival instructor covers EO M224.02 — the seven enemies of survival. These are the physical and psychological forces that erode your ability to survive: Pain, Cold, Thirst, Hunger, Fatigue, Boredom, and Loneliness. Each enemy must be recognized and actively countered. Pain and cold can be managed with first aid and shelter. Thirst and hunger require water and food sourcing. Fatigue demands rest. Boredom and loneliness are psychological enemies that can destroy your will to survive — countered by staying busy, maintaining routine, and keeping hope alive.

=== INDEX 78 ===
Your survival instructor covers EO M224.03 — predicting weather using cloud formations. Weather awareness is critical for survival: knowing when a storm is approaching lets you prepare shelter, secure your fire, and avoid dangerous exposure. You will learn to identify cloud types and what they signal: cirrus clouds (high, wispy, fair weather but may indicate change), cumulus clouds (fluffy, fair weather), cumulonimbus clouds (towering, thunderstorms), stratus clouds (flat grey layer, steady rain), and nimbostratus (dark grey, continuous rain). Natural signs like bird behavior, insect activity, and wind direction changes also provide weather clues.

=== INDEX 79 ===
Your survival instructor covers EO M224.04 — emergency shelters. Shelter is your protection from the elements and is a top survival priority. You will learn the difference between natural shelters (tree hollow, cave, root shelter) and improvised shelters (bough shelter, fallen trunk, lean-to, snow cave, tepee). Critical site selection factors include: elevated ground with good drainage, wind protection, proximity to water and fuel, availability of building materials, away from animal trails, visible from the air for rescue, enough time before nightfall, and entrance oriented leeward (away from prevailing wind). A well-built shelter can mean the difference between life and death.

=== INDEX 80 ===
Your survival instructor covers EO M224.05 — fire. Fire provides warmth, light, morale, a way to purify water, cook food, and signal for rescue. The fire triangle is the foundation: Air (oxygen), Heat, and Fuel — remove any one and fire cannot exist. You will learn the four fuel types in order of use: Tinder (fine, fluffy, catches spark easily), Kindling (small sticks, pencil-thin), Wood Fuel (thumb to wrist-thick branches), and Bulk Firewood (larger logs for sustained heat). Fire lays include the tepee, crossbed, and crisscross (pyramid). You must also check the CFFDRS fire danger rating and always use a fire ring or pit for safety.

=== INDEX 81 ===
Your survival instructor covers EO M224.06 — signalling for rescue. Once your immediate survival needs are met (shelter, fire, water), signalling becomes the path home. The universal distress signal is THREE of anything — three whistle blasts, three mirror flashes, three fires in a row. You will learn the signalling devices: flares, signal mirrors, whistles, radios and cell phones, and fire/smoke signals. Ground-to-air signals are symbols marked on the ground (each 1m wide by 8m long, placed 3m apart) that aircraft can read. Three fires arranged in a triangle 35 metres apart is an internationally recognized distress signal visible from the air.

=== INDEX 82 ===
Your survival instructor covers foraging awareness. Food is the lowest survival priority — you can survive weeks without it — but it provides energy and morale. The golden rule of foraging is absolute: if you cannot identify a plant with 100% certainty, do not eat it. Many poisonous plants look similar to edible ones. You will learn to identify common safe plants, recognize dangerous ones, and understand the universal edibility test for uncertain plants. Berries, roots, nuts, and certain greens can supplement survival rations, but mistakes can be fatal. When in doubt, go without.

=== INDEX 83 ===
Your survival instructor covers water sourcing and purification. You can survive only 3 days without water, making it a critical survival priority after shelter. You will learn to locate water by observing animal tracks, bird flight patterns, vegetation, and natural drainage. Water must always be purified before drinking — even clear mountain streams can contain harmful bacteria, parasites, and contaminants. The two primary purification methods are boiling (rolling boil for at least 1 minute, 3+ minutes at altitude) and chemical purification tablets. Improvised filtration using layers of sand, charcoal, and cloth removes sediment but does NOT kill bacteria — always follow filtration with boiling or chemical treatment.

=== INDEX 84 ===
Your survival instructor has cleared you to begin the Bushcraft & Survival training rotation. Work through each performance objective in sequence. Each mission is a hands-on survival simulation — you will apply the S.T.O.P. procedure, build emergency shelters, light and maintain fires, purify water, identify safe forage, and signal for rescue just like a real cadet operating in the wilderness during a survival exercise.

=== INDEX 85 ===
Your survival instructor introduces the foundational survival psychology module. When you realize you are lost or stranded, the first action is always S.T.O.P. — Sit, Think, Observe, Plan. Panic is the biggest enemy. You will learn the five elements of survival (Attitude, Shelter, Water, Fire, Food) and the rule of three: you can survive 3 minutes without air, 3 hours without shelter in extreme conditions, 3 days without water, and 3 weeks without food. Understanding these priorities is what keeps survivors alive.
