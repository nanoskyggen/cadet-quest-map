How to Play — Expedition Trail

The Mission

You lead a three-day backcountry expedition across nine waypoints. At each waypoint a field decision appears — read the conditions and pick the best action. Each decision tests a real expedition skill: kit packing, campsite assessment, weather reading, fire safety, water purification, knots, navigation, leave-no-trace, wildlife, and emergency response.

Survival Stats

You manage four survival priorities shown as bars on the right:

• Energy — your physical reserves. Drops with exertion and bad decisions. Low energy means fatigue and slower recovery.

• Hydration — water balance. Drops steadily each waypoint and faster in heat. Dehydration saps energy and morale.

• Warmth — body temperature. Drops in cold, wet, and wind. Good layering and shelter restore it.

• Morale — team spirit. Good decisions and reaching waypoints lift it; mistakes and low stats drag it down.

Choosing an Action

Each scenario offers 2–4 choices. One is the best fieldcraft answer. Correct choices earn points, restore or conserve stats, and advance you to the next waypoint. Wrong choices cost points and drain stats. Some choices are acceptable but not ideal — they carry smaller penalties.

Stat Danger Zone

If any stat drops to 0, your expedition takes a serious hit — you may lose a day, take a large score penalty, or be forced into an emergency stop. Keep all four bars above zero to stay mission-capable.

Scoring

• +40 per correct decision. • −15 per wrong decision. • +15 bonus per day completed with all stats safe. • Reaching the final waypoint completes the expedition with a completion bonus. Earned XP also feeds into your overall field training rank.

Weather & Terrain

Weather changes each day and affects which stats drain fastest. Read the day strip at the top — rain drains warmth, heat drains hydration, cold drains warmth and energy. The terrain on the map shows forest, water, and open ground that influence your decisions.

How to Play — Patrol Range

Mission

You command a four-cadet section on patrol. Enemy sentries patrol the terrain and will detect any cadet who breaks the principles of why things are seen. Your task is to move the section to each objective undetected using silent field signals.

How to Direct a Cadet

1. Click a friendly contact (green dot) on the tactical map to select it. Its data block appears in the control panel.

2. Build a field signal by clicking chips in order: callsign → hand signal → direction/detail.

3. Send the signal. The cadet follows your order and moves across the terrain.

Movement Techniques

• Leopard Crawl — slowest, lowest profile, hardest to detect. Use in open ground.

• Monkey Run — medium speed, crouched. Good for partial cover.

• Ghost Walk — fast, upright. Only safe in dead ground or behind solid cover.

• Halt — freeze in place. Drops your detection risk to zero while still.

Field Signals

• Advance + direction — move the cadet in that compass direction.

• Halt — stop and freeze; concealment improves while still.

• Take Cover — dash to nearest cover/concealment marker.

• Observe — hold position and scan; can spot nearby sentries.

Why Things Are Seen

Sentries detect cadets who show shape (silhouette against sky), shine (reflective gear), shadow, silhouette, movement, spacing (regular intervals look unnatural), or surface (contrasting colour). Moving fast in the open is the quickest way to be detected. Use cover, move slowly, and choose the right technique for the ground.

Scoring

• +50 per cadet reaching an objective undetected.

• +10 per successful observation of a sentry.

• −20 per detection (cadet compromised).

• Complete all four cadets to advance the wave and spawn a harder patrol.
