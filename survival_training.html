How to Play — Bush Survivor

Your Mission

You are a cadet stranded in the Canadian wilderness. Your goal is to survive and get rescued. Manage four vital stats — Warmth, Hydration, Energy, and Morale — and increase your Rescue percentage by signalling for help. If any vital stat hits zero, your survival attempt ends.

Choose a Location

Each Canadian location has different weather, temperature, terrain, and wildlife. The Arctic is brutally cold; the Atlantic coast offers water but risks hypothermia; the boreal forest has abundant resources. Pick your scenario from the location bar above the scene.

Survival Actions

Click action buttons to survive. Each action costs energy and advances time:

  • 🏕️ Build Shelter — Protects you from weather and slows warmth loss. Essential first step.
  • 🔥 Build Fire — Provides warmth, boils water, cooks food, and lifts morale. Needs tinder and fuel.
  • 💧 Find Water — Gather water from streams, snow, or rain. Then purify it.
  • 🫖 Purify Water — Boil water (needs fire) or use purification tablets. Unsafe water can make you sick.
  • 🍎 Forage Food — Find berries, roots, or catch fish. Restores energy. Some locations are richer.
  • 🚶 Rest — Conserve energy and recover. Best done in a shelter with a fire.
  • 🆘 Signal Rescue — Build a ground-to-air signal (V, X, or smoke column). Increases rescue chance.
  • 🧭 Navigate — Attempt to walk out. Risky but can find roads or settlements. Costs heavy energy.

Vital Stats

Warmth drops in cold weather and at night — fire and shelter protect it. Hydration drops over time — drink purified water. Energy is spent on every action — rest and food restore it. Morale affects recovery — fire, food, and shelter lift it; cold and isolation lower it.

Getting Rescued

Signal for rescue to raise your Rescue percentage. When it reaches 100%, a search aircraft spots you and the mission is a success! Different signals have different effectiveness — a smoke column on a clear day is highly visible, while a ground signal works best in open terrain.

Watch Out

Weather changes dynamically — storms, snow, and fog reduce rescue visibility and drain warmth fast. Night falls and temperatures drop. Wildlife may appear. Listen to the ambient sounds — wind, fire crackling, water, birds, and even a wolf howl at night. Stay alive long enough and help will come!
